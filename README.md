# Aeronautical Map Master

The deck contains 125 curated ICAO Annex 4 Appendix 2 chart symbols and 25 ICAO Annex 14 Volume I physical airport markings, signs, and lights. The Annex 4 set includes 25 aerodrome/heliport and obstacle-chart symbols (Appendix 2, APP 2-20); the Annex 14 set depicts physical airport features, not chart symbols. The selection is not statistically ranked by frequency. Each card displays its source, applicable standard section, version notes, and compliance limitations.

The accessible FOCA-hosted Annex 4 copy is the 11th Edition (July 2009). Its amendment record indicates Amendment 62, while the file label marks the consolidation as in progress. ICAO's official listing includes Amendments 57–63, but Amendment 63 text was not independently inspected. The deck therefore does not claim current consolidated Annex 4 compliance.

The Annex 14 reference is a public copy of Volume I, 9th Edition (July 2022), hosted by Switzerland's Federal Office of Civil Aviation (FOCA). Its pages include Amendment 18 and Corrigendum 3 markings; Corrigendum 3 is dated 8 June 2026. ICAO Doc 8697 is listed as the 3rd Edition (2016), with Corrigendum 1 and Amendment 1 dated 11 December 2020. Its full text was not independently inspected, so no provision-level Doc 8697 compliance claim is made. These flashcards are for study only, not an approved source for navigation, ATC, flight planning, chart production, or aerodrome certification.

Each card includes a per-symbol audit note and source/version details. The 125 Annex 4 drawings were compared with the cited Appendix 2 figures in the accessible 11th Edition (July 2009) copy; notes identify simplified geometry and omitted variants. The 25 Annex 14 drawings were individually checked against their cited provisions in the accessible FOCA-hosted copy, including the depicted feature and colour. The audit replaced a non-standard taxiway-edge-marking card with a runway turn-pad marking and corrected the NO ENTRY sign artwork. Annex 14 cards remain schematic study redrawings, not official source artwork, dimensional installation drawings, or a full operational-compliance determination; site-specific applicability and State differences are not assessed. Amendment 63 to Annex 4 and the full Doc 8697 text were not independently inspected.

The supplied Doc 4444 (16th Edition, 2016, Amendments 1–7-A incorporated, 466 pp.) and Doc 9432 (4th Edition, 2007, 102 pp.) copies concern air traffic management and radiotelephony, not chart-symbol legends, so they are not used as authority for any card. The ICAO Store lists Doc 4444 (16th Edition) up to Amendment 14 dated 26 Nov 2026, so the supplied copy is outdated; Doc 9432's 4th Edition (2007) is still the edition on sale. The supplied ICAO Quick Reference to Phraseology (20 pp.) carries no edition date and likewise supplies no chart symbols. The CAA radio manual and quick-reference phraseology sheet require confirmation of their issuing authority, jurisdiction, and current edition; the EVAIR 2015–19 bulletin is historical safety material. PDFCoffee search listings included provider-specific Jeppesen, Navtech, and Navblue legends and an uploaded Doc 10066 copy. Those listings do not establish an official, current, or universally applicable source and were not used to validate this app. ICAO identifies State AIPs, together with their amendments and supplements, as the primary source for aeronautical information; use the current State AIP, chart-specific legend, applicable regulations, and approved operational manuals instead.

Research context: Google Scholar's search pages returned a robot-verification challenge during this review. Publication metadata and abstracts were cross-checked through OpenAlex and the linked DOI or repository records. A 2026 systematic review of 36 flight-deck spatial-visualization papers reports that most user studies were conducted in labs or simulators, with only a handful in real-world environments; it also discusses cognitive load and attentional tunneling. A 2007 instrument-rated-pilot study found most proposed electronic-chart symbols were recognized, but a few were problematic. A 2019 study surveyed 229 rated pilots about electronic-chart information needs, while a 2008 industry survey documents variation among avionics manufacturers and chart providers. These papers provide human-factors context, not validation of this app or authority for operational use.

The Quick Card Check presents one symbol with two possible definitions, gives immediate feedback, and awards one mark for a correct answer before the user moves to the quiz. It can shuffle to another symbol while keeping a running score.

The quiz supports identification and multi-pair matching. Easy quizzes use one aerodrome group, Medium mixes two, and Hard uses all three; each mode offers configurable lengths and Versions A–C. Questions and choices are shuffled, and each correct answer or pair earns one mark.
